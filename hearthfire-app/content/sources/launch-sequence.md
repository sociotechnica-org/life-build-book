
HEARTHFIRE
Level 0: The First 48 Hours
Launch Sequence & Facilitation Guide

Parent-Facing Operations Manual
March 2026 • v1.0

Design Principle: Do, then learn. Every mechanic is introduced because the player just did something that triggers it. No front-loading. No manuals.



The Orientation Problem
The instinct is to explain the system before launching it. Here’s how XP works, here’s the gear catalog, here’s the level structure... This is a mistake, especially with ADHD. Manuals are where engagement goes to die.
Level 0 is not an orientation briefing. It’s a guided first run where each step demonstrates a mechanic by having the player use it. By the end of the first 48 hours, he’s not reading about the system — he’s already inside it.

What he learns by doing in the first 48 hours:
• Morning check-in → self-reporting
• XP → actions have measurable value
• Gaming gate → cause and effect
• Foraging → variable rewards
• Inventory → accumulation toward goals
• Gear catalog → materials build real things
• Evening reflection → the second loop
• Quests → bigger challenges with choice


What He Does NOT Learn Yet
These mechanics don’t exist in his world until the system introduces them naturally:
Momentum (introduced Day 4–5, when there’s enough data for a score), Weekly calibration (introduced first Sunday), Car access (introduced when weekly activation becomes relevant — see forward-unlock note below), Domain proficiency (emergent, not explained).

Car Access: The Forward-Unlock
He has passed his driver’s test but doesn’t have his license yet. This is actually a better design than having access from Day 1. The framing:
"You’re earning car access before you even have the license. When it arrives, you won’t be starting from zero — you’ll already be qualified."
This means Level 1 car privileges (errand runs, defined destinations) are banked. The moment the license is in hand, he’s already cleared the gate. No waiting period, no probation — he pre-earned it.

Track car-access qualification on the weekly check-in from Week 1, even though it can’t be used yet. Seeing the qualification status build before the license arrives creates anticipation and reinforces the responsibility → autonomy link.

Pre-Launch Night
This is the only briefing moment, and it should be short. Under 10 minutes. The goal is not to explain the system. The goal is to create one clear commitment for tomorrow morning.

1
Set the Wake Time Together
Agree on tomorrow’s target wake time. Write it down. This is a contract, not a suggestion.


2
Hand Him the Dawn March Card
"Tomorrow morning, this is your mission. The alarm is your responsibility. When you’re up and ready, bring this card to the kitchen table. That’s it."


3
Set the Alarm Together
His phone, his alarm, his responsibility. You are not the backup. If he asks about consequences for missing it, keep it simple: "We’ll figure out the rest tomorrow. Tonight, just set the alarm."


FACILITATOR NOTE: Do NOT explain XP, foraging, gear, levels, quests, or car access tonight. He doesn’t need to understand the system. He needs to wake up tomorrow and do one thing. Resist the urge to sell it.


What he knows after tonight:
1. There’s a wake time.
2. There’s a card.
3. Bring it to the kitchen.



Day 1 Morning: The Dawn March
He wakes up. He does the card — alarm off, feet on floor, meds, comes to the kitchen. This is the solo piece. You are not waking him up.
When he arrives at the kitchen table, that’s when the system comes alive. Walk through five micro-moments, each introducing one mechanic. Total time at the table: roughly 10 minutes.

1
The Check-In
Hand him the morning check-in sheet. "Let’s fill this out." Walk through each item together the first time. He scores himself.
Don’t correct or verify. If he says he did all hygiene and you can see he didn’t shower, let it go. The system is teaching self-assessment, and patterns become visible over time.
→ Mechanic learned: Self-reporting. The check-in is a mirror, not a test.


2
The XP Reveal
"See these points? That’s XP — experience points. You just earned some." Show him the XP tracker. He writes his first number.
Keep it concrete: "Morning check-in: 10 base XP, plus 2 for each item you nailed. You got [X] today."
→ Mechanic learned: XP exists. Actions generate measurable value.


3
The Gaming Gate
"You scored [X] out of 8. Threshold is 5. Gaming’s unlocked today."
If he didn’t hit 5 (unlikely on Day 1 with you guiding, but possible): "Not today. Tomorrow’s a fresh check-in." No lecture. No negotiation. The number is the number.
→ Mechanic learned: Check-in → access. Simple cause and effect.


4
The First Forage
"The check-in also triggered something else. Let’s see what you found." This is where the foraging mechanic fires for the first time — he draws a card, rolls, or uses the digital tool.
He gets a material (Hearthstone or Ironwood at Level 1) or a provision. React to whatever he gets: "Nice, Ironwood. That’s useful." Don’t over-explain the rarity system.
→ Mechanic learned: Variable rewards. Engagement generates loot.


5
The Inventory
"That material goes here." He places it on the inventory tracker. First mark in the first box.
Don’t show the gear catalog yet. Just: "You’ll see what these build toward tonight." Create the gap.
→ Mechanic learned: Materials accumulate. They’re building toward something.


FACILITATOR NOTE: That’s it for morning. Five mechanics, all experienced through doing, each one taking about 30 seconds to land. He goes about his day. The system is silent until evening.



Day 1 Evening: The Night Watch
Before gaming winds down for the night (or before bed), the second loop fires.

6
Evening Check-In
Hand him the Night Watch card and the evening check-in sheet. He fills it out: room status, tomorrow prep, calendar check, plus the reflection questions.
The reflection prompts matter: "What worked today?" and "What got in the way?" These are coaching questions. Let him answer without editing.
→ Mechanic learned: The evening loop exists. Reflection is part of the deal.


7
Second Forage
"Evening check-in triggers a forage too." He draws/rolls again. Second material lands in inventory.
→ Mechanic learned: Engagement → loot, twice daily. The loop reinforces itself.


8
The Gear Catalog Reveal
Now — and only now — show him the gear catalog handout.
"See these materials you’re collecting? This is what they build toward." Let him look. Let him find the items he wants. Let him do the mental math: "I need 12 Hearthstone for the LED strip... I got 1 today... so about 6 more days..."
This is the moment where the accumulation mechanic clicks. The materials aren’t abstract — they’re building toward a phone mount, a cooling pad, LED lights for his room.
→ Mechanic learned: Materials → gear. Accumulation has a destination he chose.


Day 1 Complete. He has experienced:
• Morning check-in + evening check-in (both loops)
• XP earning and tracking
• Gaming access gate
• Foraging (x2)
• Inventory tracking
• Gear catalog — knows what he’s building toward


All by doing. Zero lectures. Zero manuals.

Day 2 Morning: The Repeat
Same Dawn March. Same check-in. Same forage. But now it’s familiar — he’s done it before. The system starts to feel like a loop, not an event.

9
XP Accumulation
The only new thing: "Yesterday you earned [X] XP. Today you’re at [X + today’s]. Here’s where that puts you on the tracker."
The XP number growing is the mechanic doing its job. Visible accumulation.
→ Mechanic learned: XP accumulates visibly. Progress compounds.


FACILITATOR NOTE: Day 2 morning should take less facilitation than Day 1. He knows the forms, he knows the sequence. Start stepping back. If he fills it out and asks "did I get gaming?" — great. He’s internalized the gate.


Day 2 Evening: Quest Introduction
After the evening check-in and forage (which should now feel routine), introduce the next layer.

10
The Quest Reveal
"You’ve got the daily loop down. Ready for something bigger?"
Show him 2–3 Level 1 quest options. Let him read them. Let him choose.
Suggested first-quest options (pick 2–3 that match his current situation):
• Space Audit — Take photos of your room, identify 3 problem zones
• Morning Map — Write out your actual morning routine with real times
• Time Detective — Estimate how long 5 tasks take, then measure them
• Med Mapping — Document your meds: what, when, why, what happens if missed


11
Immediate XP for Commitment
"Picking a quest earns you 10 XP right now, just for committing." He picks. You add the XP.
This is deliberate — initiative is rewarded immediately. The quest itself earns more XP on completion, with bonus for reflection quality, but the act of choosing has value.
→ Mechanic learned: Quests exist. You choose them. Initiative has instant value.


FACILITATOR NOTE: Don’t explain reflection quality tiers yet. When he finishes the quest and writes his reflection, you can introduce the idea that deeper thinking earns more XP. Let him write a natural reflection first, then show him what “deep” looks like. Do, then learn.

Edge Cases & What-Ifs
He doesn’t wake up on Day 1
Don’t wake him. When he does get up, run the check-in normally. He scores himself honestly (late = 0 for wake-up). If he doesn’t hit 5/8, no gaming today. No lecture. "Tomorrow’s a fresh check-in." The system teaches the lesson. You don’t have to.

He inflates his self-report
Let it go. This is by design. Self-assessment accuracy is a skill that develops over time. The pattern becomes visible through quest outcomes, calibration walks, and his own growing awareness. Correcting him on Day 1 turns the check-in back into surveillance.

He’s not interested in the foraging
Unlikely given the variable reward psychology, but if it doesn’t land, don’t force it. Run the forage, note what he got, move on. The gear catalog reveal in the evening usually activates interest retroactively. If not, the system works without foraging — it’s an engagement booster, not a core mechanic.

He wants to know everything about the system immediately
Good sign — that’s his educator brain. Give him the big picture verbally: "There are 7 levels, 8 skill domains, quests get harder as you go, car access expands as you level up." But don’t hand him the Design Bible. Let mechanics continue to reveal through play. He’ll design with you starting Level 3–4.

He resists the evening check-in
The evening check-in has no gate — it doesn’t block anything. It earns XP and foraging. If he skips it, he just progresses slower. Point that out without pressure: "Your call. It’s 15 XP and a forage you’re leaving on the table." Let the system’s incentive structure do the work.
